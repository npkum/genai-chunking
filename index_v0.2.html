A key challenge in LLM applications is the limitation of the context window. The models can only effectively analyze and reason over a limited amount of text at a time. This constraint affects their performance on tasks that require a broader contextual understanding, such as semantic search and document summarization.

Chunking presents a viable solution to this issue. This process involves breaking down large text into smaller, more manageable segments. By doing so, we can provide information to the LLM within its optimal processing capacity. The role of chunking in LLM applications focuses on its effects on retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) performance and its practical uses in the real world.

We will explore how the size of chunks influences these tasks, noting that smaller chunks are advantageous for searching specific concepts and keywords, while larger chunks are better for grasping overall context.

Once you determine the most effective chunking strategy for your use case, the next step is selecting the best embedding technique and vector database to store the data, including its embedded metrics and index. What is Chunking

Chunking involves breaking information into smaller, manageable pieces to enhance storage and retrieval efficiency. By organizing data this way, it becomes easier for models to retrieve relevant information related to specific questions, leading to more precise and cost-effective generation of responses.

In the context of Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), chunking is essential for improving a model’s ability to understand and process complex information by transforming it into digestible segments.
